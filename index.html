What is an Odds Ratio?

The Odds Ratio (OR) quantifies the strength of association between an exposure and an outcome. An OR of 1 indicates no association; OR > 1 suggests increased odds of the outcome with exposure; OR < 1 suggests decreased odds (protective effect). It is widely used in epidemiology, clinical research, and public health.

Three Types Available

  • Crude OR (2×2 Table): Simple association between one exposure and one outcome
  • Mantel-Haenszel OR: Adjusted OR controlling for a confounding variable across strata
  • Logistic Regression OR: Adjusted ORs from multivariable binary logistic regression with multiple covariates

When to Use

  • Case-control studies (primary measure)
  • Cross-sectional surveys with binary outcomes
  • Malaria risk factor analysis
  • Treatment effectiveness with binary endpoints
  • Disease association with exposure variables
∑ MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK

Crude Odds Ratio (2×2 Table)

OR = (a × d) / (b × c)

Where a = exposed cases, b = exposed non-cases, c = unexposed cases, d = unexposed non-cases

Mantel-Haenszel Odds Ratio

ORMH = Σ(aidi/ni) / Σ(bici/ni)

Pooled OR across K strata, adjusting for the confounding variable.
i = 1, 2, ..., K strata; ni = total in stratum i

Logistic Regression Odds Ratio

log(p / (1−p)) = β₀ + β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + ... + βₖXₖ

OR for each covariate = exp(β). The logit link function models the log-odds as a linear combination of predictors.

OR
Odds Ratio
OR = 1: No association. OR > 1: Exposure increases odds (risk factor). OR < 1: Exposure decreases odds (protective). OR = 2.0: Exposed group has twice the odds. OR = 0.5: Exposed group has half the odds.
95% CI
Confidence Interval
Range of plausible values for the true OR. If the CI includes 1.0, the association is not statistically significant. Calculated using: exp(ln(OR) ± 1.96 × SE(ln(OR))). Narrow CI = precise estimate; Wide CI = need more data.
χ²
Chi-Square Test
Tests whether the association between exposure and outcome is statistically significant. Uses Pearson's chi-square or Yates' continuity correction for small samples. If p < 0.05, reject independence.
p
Fisher's Exact Test
Exact test for small samples where chi-square may be unreliable (any expected cell < 5). Calculates exact probability under the null hypothesis of no association. Preferred when sample is small.
β
Log-Odds (Logistic Reg.)
The regression coefficient in logistic regression. OR = exp(β). β > 0 means increased odds; β < 0 means decreased odds; β = 0 means no effect. Standard error of β used to compute CI and Wald test.
ORMH
Mantel-Haenszel OR
Weighted average of stratum-specific ORs that adjusts for confounding. Assumes homogeneity of ORs across strata (tested by Breslow-Day test). More efficient than crude OR when confounding is present.
📊 UNDERSTANDING YOUR RESULTS

Output Parameters Explained

OR Odds Ratio (Key Metric)

Interpretation Guide:

  • OR = 1.0: No association — exposure doesn't affect odds
  • OR = 1.5: 50% higher odds with exposure
  • OR = 2.0: 2× the odds (doubled risk)
  • OR = 3.0: 3× the odds (tripled risk)
  • OR = 0.5: 50% lower odds (protective)
  • OR = 0.25: 75% lower odds (strongly protective)
χ² Pearson Chi-Square

What it is: Tests independence between exposure and outcome

Assumptions:

  • All expected frequencies ≥ 5 (use Fisher's exact if not)
  • Independent observations

Decision: p < 0.05 → significant association

RR Risk Ratio (Bonus)

What it is: Ratio of risk in exposed vs unexposed

Formula: RR = [a/(a+b)] / [c/(c+d)]

When to use: Cohort studies and RCTs (prospective)

Note: OR ≈ RR when outcome is rare (< 10%)

BD Breslow-Day Test

What it is: Tests homogeneity of ORs across strata

H₀: ORs are equal across all strata

If p > 0.05: ORs are homogeneous → MH OR is valid

If p < 0.05: ORs differ across strata → report stratum-specific ORs or consider interaction
